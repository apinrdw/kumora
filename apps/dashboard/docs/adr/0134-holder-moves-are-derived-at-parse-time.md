# Holder moves are derived at parse time

The Holder moves page's Daily view lists every above-5% holder that moved on one trading day across the market, with its 20-day Trend, and a rail of the last 10 days' counts. Reading it on demand meant loading every moving stock's 21 trading days of blocks and accounts at once: real days already read 46,000 to 59,000 account rows (20 Nov and 12 Oct 2026, from 263 to 343 moving stocks), over 100 MiB of a Worker's 128 MiB, and 586 MiB at the parse's caps. The above-5% parse run now derives each day's moves after its parse steps, in one `derive:<date>` step per day, and stores them as lines (`above_5_move`, `holdings-migrations/0011_above_5_move.sql`); the page reads one day's lines and one count per rail day and move. A day's lines come from the stock's Daily holders view model (`lib/daily-holders.ts`) run over the stock's 21 parsed days up to that day, trimmed to the stock page's row budget (ADR 0133), so Holder moves and a stock's Daily holders can't disagree. Each derived day records its basis: the derivation's version and the version of each of the 21 parsed days it read. `derive-reconcile` picks every day whose basis differs, so a day newly parsed or parsed again re-derives itself and the 20 days after it whose Trends read it, and a parser version bump re-derives every day. A day that fails to parse keeps its rows and its basis, so its lines stay. Stocks are read in turns whose rows fit the row budget, decoded one stock at a time, and each turn's lines are staged in `above_5_move_pending`; one batch then swaps the day's staged lines in, so a reader or a failed attempt sees a day's lines whole. Through is stored as custodian names, and broker codes are still looked up on read (ADR 0132). `tests/server/holdings/holder-moves-heap.test.ts` derives a day at the caps (16,000 lines, 21 days of 8,000 rows) in a worker whose heap stops at 72 MiB, about 25 MiB of it the worker's own (it fit 64 MiB, not 56, on 25 Nov 2026), and reads it in under 32 MiB. The cost is one more step per derived day in a run, and twice the rows written for each line. Undoing this means reading moves on demand again, which needs another bound on the read, such as computing the rail's counts at parse time and reading fewer trend days. Monthly moves are still read on demand.
