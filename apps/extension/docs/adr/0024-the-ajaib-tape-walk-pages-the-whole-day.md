# The Ajaib tape walk pages the whole day at 50,000 then 49,999 rows

The walk of the newest closed day on Ajaib's REST tape (`running_trades/`) used to walk it one 3-minute block at a time at 5,000 rows a page, then walk every block that took 2+ pages again at 4,999: about 800 calls and tens of minutes, which gave a token that long to go stale. It now walks the whole day the way the owner's console collector does: one fixed `last_timestamp` (the walk's start plus one hour, since `last_timestamp` filters on a hidden time and is no cursor), `direction=DESC`, a first pass at 50,000 rows a page and a second at 49,999. Page k starts at offset (k−1)·(P+1) and holds P rows, so each pass skips one trade per page boundary, and the two passes skip different ones. The second pass posts only the trades the first pass didn't get: those at the first pass's skipped offsets, past the offset it reached, or at an offset whose row it read but didn't post (one it couldn't read, one without valid codes), save another day's row. The second pass decides by what the first posted, not by what it read. Posting every row again would re-upload a real day's ~1M trades for the ~20 its boundaries skipped. A pass ends at a page that answers fewer rows than asked or says no page follows, and at 100 pages at most. Probed on 2026-11-26 at 200,000 / 199,999: 12 calls, 130 s, every trade but the 22 IDX never printed. 50,000 and not 200,000: a 200,000-row page is about 60 MB and took 6 to 20 s, past the relay's 15 s request timeout, and that much would cross the tab-to-worker message on every page; 50,000 keeps a page near 15 MB, about 46 calls a day.

The walk keeps its position in `chrome.storage.local` after each page: `{ day, pass, page, lastTimestamp, fetched, reached, missed }` while it walks, and `{ day, pass: "done" }` once walked. `reached` and `missed` are the first pass's reached offset and the offsets before it whose row it didn't post, other than another day's: the first pass adds to them page by page, and the second pass reads them as the first left them. The walk keeps no state outside its position, so a restart at any page loses nothing the second pass's choice of trades depends on. A restarted worker resumes on the same `last_timestamp`, so its pages line up with those before the restart, its second pass posts the same trades, and its trade count carries on. A position kept mid-walk in the old block shape doesn't decode and the day is walked afresh; Production keeps the trades it already holds. Each page's rows go up as consecutive batches of at most `BATCH_ROWS` (packages/relay ADR 0012).
